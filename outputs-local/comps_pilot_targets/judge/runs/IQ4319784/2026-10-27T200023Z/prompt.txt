You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: Cricut, Inc. (cricut.com)
- Core offering: Designs and sells smart cutting and crafting machines, plus blades, mats, vinyl and iron-on materials, and a design software subscription. Sold mainly to hobbyists and small crafters, who cut and decorate their own projects using the company's software and materials.
- Primary products: smart cutting machines; blades and cutting tools; cutting mats; vinyl and iron-on materials; pens and accessories
- Primary services: design software subscription; ready-to-make project content
- Customer segment: B2C: hobbyists, DIY crafters, parents and gift makers; B2B: small business owners who make and sell custom goods
- End markets: arts and crafts; home decor; gifts and personalization; small business
- Revenue model: product sales: cutting machines, tools, blades, mats and materials sold through retail and online; subscription: Cricut Access membership for design content, fonts and images
- Value chain position: brand owner: designs machines and materials made by contract manufacturers, sold to consumers and retailers; software / platform: Design Space software and content library, sold to users by subscription and free access
- Geography served: global: US, Canada, Europe, Australia, Japan and other international markets
- Company size: large
- Product specialization: related lines: cutting machines, blades and materials, and the Cricut Access subscription, all sold to the same crafting customers
- Distribution channels: online: cricut.com and major online marketplaces; third-party retail: big-box, craft and electronics retailers such as Walmart, Target, Michaels and Best Buy
- Regulatory exposure: low: normal consumer product safety and electronics rules for powered devices and consumer products
