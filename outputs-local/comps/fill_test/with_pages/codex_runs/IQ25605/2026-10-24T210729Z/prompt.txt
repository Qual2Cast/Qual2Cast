Facets:

F1 Core offering (offering_description): What the company sells, and to whom.
  Write one or two plain sentences.
  Example: Designs and manufactures rooftop HVAC units for commercial buildings

F2 Primary products (primary_products): The main products it sells.
  Write a short text.
  Use general names, not brand names. Separate the items with semicolons. If the company sells no products, set the status to not_applicable.
  Example: HR software; medical devices; hot-mix asphalt

F3 Primary services (primary_services): The main services it provides.
  Write a short text.
  Separate the items with semicolons. If the company sells no services, set the status to not_applicable. Support that comes with a product, such as a warranty, is not a service.
  Example: consulting; freight logistics; payroll processing

F4 Customer segment (customer_segment): Who buys its products or services.
  Choose all that apply: B2B, B2C, B2G. Add a short detail for each.
  B2B means businesses and private institutions. B2C means individual consumers or patients. B2G means government agencies. In the detail, name the customer groups.
  Example: B2B: mechanical contractors, commercial building owners; B2G: school districts

F5 End markets (end_markets): The industries it serves.
  Write a short text.
  Name the industries or markets where customers use its products or services. Separate the items with semicolons. For a consumer business, name the consumer market, such as home improvement.
  Example: healthcare; retail; manufacturing

F6 Revenue model (revenue_model): How it earns money.
  Choose all that apply: product sales, subscription, transaction or usage fees, licensing or royalties, service fees, project or contract, rental or leasing, advertising, healthcare reimbursement, interest or premiums, commissions or management fees, other. Add a short detail for each.
  In the detail, say what customers pay for.
  Example: product sales: equipment sold through dealers; service fees: installation and repair

F7 Value chain position (value_chain_position): Its role in the industry.
  Choose all that apply: raw materials, component supplier, OEM / manufacturer, brand owner, distributor, retailer, service provider, software / platform, asset operator, financial intermediary, other. Add a short detail for each.
  A brand owner designs and markets products that other companies make. An asset operator owns and runs physical assets, such as plants, networks, buildings or vehicle fleets. In the detail, say what the company takes in and who it sells to.
  Example: OEM / manufacturer: builds HVAC units from purchased components, sold to contractors

F8 Geography served (geography_served): Where it sells or operates.
  Choose one: US regional, US national, North America, global. Add a short detail.
  Choose the widest area. US regional means some states or one region. In the detail, name the regions, states or countries.
  Example: US regional: Alabama, Florida, Georgia and the Carolinas

F9 Company size (company_size): How large its operations are, in words.
  Write a short text.
  Describe size with words only. Use these signs: the number of sites in words (such as a single plant, or locations across the country), the area it covers, the range of products, the type of customers, and how the company describes itself (such as family-owned, or a regional leader). Do not give numbers, counts, revenue, employee counts or percentages, in digits or in words.
  Example: regional operator with several plants and a national dealer network

F10 Product specialization (specialization): How wide its range of offerings is.
  Choose one: niche specialist, related lines, diversified. Add a short detail.
  A niche specialist has one product or service line. Related lines means several lines that share customers, technology or channels. Diversified means separate businesses with different customers. In the detail, name the lines or businesses.
  Example: related lines: HVAC units, controls and replacement parts

F11 Distribution channels (distribution_channels): How its products reach customers.
  Choose all that apply: direct sales, distributors / dealers, own locations, third-party retail, online, partners / resellers, agents / brokers, other. Add a short detail for each.
  In the detail, say who or what the channel is.
  Example: distributors / dealers: independent HVAC dealers; direct sales: national accounts

F12 Regulatory exposure (regulatory_exposure): How much regulation controls the business.
  Choose one: high, medium, low. Add a short detail.
  High means the company needs approval or a license to operate or sell, as for utilities, hospitals, drugs and medical devices, banks, insurers and airlines. Medium means important industry rules apply, such as environmental, safety, transport or data privacy rules. Low means only normal business rules apply. In the detail, name the regulated activity and the main regulators or rules.
  Example: high: medical devices that need FDA clearance

Also write "notes": one sentence about gaps or doubts, or leave it empty.

About Us | BioMarin
BioMarin is a global biotechnology company that relentlessly pursues bold science to translate genetic discoveries into new medicines that advance the future of human health.
As we have looked to expand our impact, our approach to cutting-edge science has remained the same. We enlist the best of the best – people with the right technical expertise and a relentless drive to solve real problems – and create an environment that empowers our teams to pursue bold, innovative science. Our work is deeply rooted in genetic insights, meaning we target the underlying genetic changes or proximal molecular mechanisms of disease.
With this distinctive approach to drug discovery, we’ve produced a diverse pipeline of commercial, clinical and preclinical candidates that have well-understood biology and provide an opportunity to be first-to-market or offer a substantial benefit over existing therapeutic options.
Key Moments in Our History
BioMarin was founded in Novato, California, with an initial focus on leveraging its proprietary enzyme technology to develop therapies for genetic conditions and other diseases.
BioMarin initiated its first clinical trial to evaluate a potential treatment a form of mucopolysaccharidosis.
Since a large number of early investors were based in Europe, the company was listed on both the Swiss SWX Exchange and the Nasdaq National Market.
First Medicine Approved for a Form of MPS
The FDA granted marketing authorization for BioMarin’s enzyme replacement therapy for the treatment of a form of mucopolysaccharidosis. The medicine was approved in Europe that June.
The FDA approved BioMarin’s first independently developed and commercialized medicine, and the first authorized treatment for a second form of mucopolysaccharidosis.
The company established BioMarin Europe Ltd., headquartered in London, initially with offices in Spain, Switzerland and Italy. The company now operates in multiple countries around the world.
First Therapy for A Rare Metabolic Condition Approved
The FDA approved the first therapy for the treatment of a rare metabolic condition.
The company completed the purchase of a manufacturing facility in Shanbally, Ireland, and in November, the FDA approved the expansion of BioMarin’s biologics manufacturing facility in Novato, California.
Licensing of Gene Therapy Program for a Bleeding Disorder
BioMarin announced it had licensed the program from University College London and St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital. This would lead to a product approval approximately 10 years later.
Third Medicine for MPS Approved
The FDA approved BioMarin’s therapy for the treatment of another form of mucopolysaccharidosis.
Therapy for a Form of Batten Disease Approved
The FDA approved BioMarin’s medicine for the treatment of an ultra-rare form of Batten disease.
Next-Generation Therapy for a Rare Metabolic Condition Approved
The FDA approved the first enzyme replacement therapy to target the underlying cause of a rare metabolic condition.
Therapy for a Form of Skeletal Dysplasia Approved
The FDA approved BioMarin’s therapy for the treatment a form of skeletal dysplasia. It was the first medicine authorized for the treatment of this growth-related condition.
Gene Therapy for Inheritable Bleeding Disorder Approved in Europe
The European Commission approved BioMarin’s first gene therapy for the treatment of an inheritable bleeding disorder.
Gene Therapy for Inheritable Bleeding Disorder Approved by FDA
The FDA approved BioMarin’s gene therapy for the treatment of an inheritable bleeding disorder.
With the retirement of longtime BioMarin CEO Jean-Jacques Bienaimé, Alexander Hardy was named President and Chief Executive Officer.
FDA Expands Approval of BioMarin’s Medicine for Form of Batten Disease for Children Under 3
The expanded indication included children of all ages living with the ultra-rare form of Batten disease, enabling even younger children to receive the only available treatment for this debilitating and ultimately fatal condition.
The acquisition strengthened BioMarin’s enzyme therapies portfolio, adding a late-stage investigational enzyme replacement therapy for ENPP1 deficiency.
FDA Expands Approval of BioMarin’s Medicine for a Rare Metabolic Condition to Adolescents
The FDA approved BioMarin’s supplemental Biologics License Application to include people 12 years of age and older living with a rare metabolic condition.
The acquisition added two new treatments for rare lysosomal storage diseases to the company’s commercial portfolio, opening up more possibilities for people living with the conditions around the world to access therapy due to BioMarin’s global footprint.
