The Bay-Walton Sector Plan is a Long-Term Master Plan on 110,500 acres of the company’s land holdings located in Bay County and Walton County. The framework was finalized after extensive public participation at workshops, open houses, and public hearings -2015. The plan was approved by Bay County, Walton County, and the State of Florida pursuant to Section 163.3245, Florida Statues.
An integral component of the Bay-Walton Sector Plan is the creation of a regionally significant water-shed level environmental framework that contains 53,229 acres or 48 percent of the master plan in a series of well-connected conservation and preservation areas, especially for the protection of West Bay.

The St. Joe Company is a diversified real estate development company headquartered in Panama City Beach, FL. With landholdings primarily concentrated in five Northwest Florida counties, St. Joe develops its land for residential, commercial and hospitality assets. Its history and its employees firmly rooted in Northwest Florida, St. Joe has adopted a long-term vision for the region, thinking not only of short-term gains but lasting benefits.
For St. Joe, development is about more than building on land—it’s about creating meaningful connections between people and place. Every project is designed to foster opportunities for community, whether through walkable town centers, welcoming accommodations, or thoughtfully planned neighborhoods. By blending natural beauty with purposeful design, we aim to create environments where people can truly belong, engage, connect and thrive.

Alfred I. duPont visited Florida seeking a vacation home. His interest soon turned to large-scale timberland acquisitions across Northwest Florida. DuPont's holdings expanded to nearly 100,000 acres of newly cleared forestlands and DuPont acquired an additional 240,000 acres in Northwest Florida, including Port St. Joe.
St. Joe is Founded - Timberland and Paper
Alfred I. duPont passed away, leaving his estate and holdings to his brother-in-law and business partner, Edward Ball. Ball incorporated the St. Joe Paper Company, continuing duPont's vision. Operations began at the paper mill in Port St. Joe with a focus on timberland and paper production. Over the next 20 years, the company acquired vast tracts of timerland, reaching more than 1 million acres.
Railroad & Sugarcane Operation Acquired
Jacob Belin, Chair of the St. Joe Paper Company, was elected as Chair of the Board of the duPont Trust after Ball’s death. Belin began selling off the company's industrial operations and shifted the focus to land development, positioning the company for the real estate boom of decades to come. The St. Joe Real Estate division was formed, seeking "the highest and best use" of St. Joe lands.
Focus Shifts from Timber to Development
the Port St. Joe paper mill was sold, shifting focus from timber to land development to maximize the company's landholdings. The following year, former Disney executive Peter Rummell became CEO and renamed the company, The St. Joe Company.
Assets Sold to Focus on Development
As the company continued to hone its focus on real estate development, it sold Talisman Sugar followed by more than 500,000 acres of land for conservation to The Nature Conservancy and the State of Florida to the early 2000s.
Development Begins on WaterColor® Community
construction began on the WaterColor 499-acre master-planned community in South Walton County, including WaterColor Inn & Resort. This was the company's first hotel project and featured 67 guestrooms and suites just steps from a private beach. The hotel is still operational today and a favorite of 30A visitors.
