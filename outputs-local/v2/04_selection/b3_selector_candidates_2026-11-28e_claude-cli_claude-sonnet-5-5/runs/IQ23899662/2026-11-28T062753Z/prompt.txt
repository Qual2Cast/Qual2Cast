You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

- Primary products: none
- Primary services: international money transfers; foreign currency exchange; bill payment and related payment services
- Customer segment: B2C: immigrant consumers sending money to family abroad
- End markets: remittances; consumer financial services; cross-border payments
- Revenue model: transaction or usage fees: fees per money transfer; other: foreign exchange spread on currency conversion
- Value chain position: financial intermediary: takes consumer funds via agents and digital channels and delivers payouts through bank and cash-pickup partners
- Geography served: global: Send from US, Canada, Europe and other countries, receive in Mexico, Central America, Caribbean, Africa and Asia
- Product specialization: niche specialist: consumer remittance and related money transfer services
- Distribution channels: agents / brokers: independent agent locations such as grocery and convenience stores; online: mobile app and website transfers; own locations: company-owned stores
- Regulatory exposure: high: licensed money transmitter subject to state licensing, FinCEN, CFPB, anti-money-laundering and foreign regulators
- Sub-sector: consumer cross-border remittances to Latin America
- People-delivered: no
