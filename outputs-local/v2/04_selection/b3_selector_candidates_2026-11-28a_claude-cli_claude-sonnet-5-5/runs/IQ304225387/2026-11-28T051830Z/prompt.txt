You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: Planet Fitness, Inc. (www.planetfitness.com)
- Core offering: Franchises and operates high-volume, low-price gyms branded as Planet Fitness, offering membership access to cardio and strength equipment in a casual atmosphere. Revenue comes mainly from franchisees' royalties and fees, plus equipment sales to franchisees and some company-owned clubs.
- Primary products: fitness equipment sold to franchisees; branded merchandise
- Primary services: gym memberships; franchising; franchise support and marketing
- Customer segment: B2C: individual gym members, mostly casual and first-time gym users; B2B: franchisees who open and operate clubs
- End markets: health and fitness; consumer wellness
- Revenue model: commissions or management fees: franchise royalties and fees from franchisee-operated clubs, plus national advertising fund contributions; product sales: fitness equipment sold to franchisees for new and re-equipped clubs; subscription: monthly membership dues at company-owned clubs
- Value chain position: brand owner: owns the Planet Fitness brand and licenses it to franchisees; asset operator: operates company-owned gyms serving members; distributor: supplies purchased equipment to franchisees
- Geography served: global: United States, Canada, Puerto Rico, Dominican Republic, Panama, Mexico, Australia, Spain
- Product specialization: related lines: franchising, company-owned clubs and equipment sales, all within the budget gym business
- Distribution channels: own locations: company-owned clubs; partners / resellers: franchisee-operated clubs; online: online membership sign-up and app
- Regulatory exposure: medium: franchise disclosure rules (FTC), consumer membership contract and auto-renewal laws, health and safety rules
- Sub-sector: franchised low-cost health club and gym memberships
- Recurring revenue: yes
