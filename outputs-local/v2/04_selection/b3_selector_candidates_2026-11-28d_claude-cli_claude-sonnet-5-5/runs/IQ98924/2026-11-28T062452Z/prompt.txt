You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

It also runs logistics assets and sells fuel through retail stations in Hawaii and the Pacific Northwest, to wholesale, commercial and consumer customers.

- End markets: transportation fuels; aviation; marine; road construction; consumer retail fuel
- Revenue model: product sales: refined petroleum products sold wholesale and at retail stations; service fees: logistics, pipeline and terminal throughput and storage
- Value chain position: OEM / manufacturer: refines purchased crude oil into fuels, sold to wholesale and commercial buyers; retailer: operates fuel stations and convenience stores selling to motorists; asset operator: runs pipelines, terminals and storage supporting refineries
- Geography served: US regional: Hawaii, Washington, Wyoming, Montana, Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountain states
- Product specialization: related lines: refining, logistics and retail fuel sales along the same fuel value chain
- Distribution channels: direct sales: wholesale and commercial contracts with airlines, distributors and industrial users; own locations: company-operated retail fuel stations in Hawaii and the Pacific Northwest; other: pipelines and terminals delivering to customers, spot and rack sales
- Regulatory exposure: high: refineries face EPA, state environmental, OSHA, renewable fuel standard and fuel-quality rules, plus pipeline safety regulation
- Sub-sector: independent petroleum refining and fuel marketing; asphalt; logistics
- Cyclical demand: no
