You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: Peloton Interactive, Inc. (www.onepeloton.com)
- Core offering: Designs and sells internet-connected exercise equipment such as stationary bikes, treadmills and rowers, along with subscription access to live and on-demand fitness classes. Sells to individual consumers and to commercial customers like gyms and hotels, through its own showrooms, website and retail partners. Equipment is made by contract manufacturers and content is produced in its own studios.
- Primary products: connected stationary bikes; connected treadmills; connected rowers; strength training equipment; refurbished equipment
- Primary services: digital fitness class subscriptions; equipment delivery and installation; equipment repair and maintenance
- Customer segment: B2C: home fitness consumers buying equipment and subscriptions; B2B: hotels, gyms, corporate and residential building fitness centers
- End markets: home fitness; wellness; hospitality; commercial fitness facilities
- Revenue model: product sales: connected fitness equipment and accessories, including apparel; subscription: monthly all-access and app memberships for classes; rental or leasing: equipment rental and financing options; service fees: delivery, installation and repair
- Value chain position: brand owner: designs equipment made by contract manufacturers, sold to consumers and businesses; software / platform: operates streaming fitness app and content platform for subscribers; retailer: sells directly through own showrooms and website
- Geography served: global: US, Canada, UK, Germany, Australia and other markets, with US the largest
- Product specialization: related lines: connected fitness hardware, digital class subscriptions and apparel/accessories
- Distribution channels: online: own website and app sales; own locations: company showrooms; third-party retail: large retailers and resale partners such as Amazon, Dick's, Costco; direct sales: commercial sales to hotels, gyms and buildings
- Regulatory exposure: medium: Consumer product safety rules (CPSC recalls), data privacy, music licensing and subscription/consumer protection laws
- Sub-sector: connected fitness equipment and subscription workout content
