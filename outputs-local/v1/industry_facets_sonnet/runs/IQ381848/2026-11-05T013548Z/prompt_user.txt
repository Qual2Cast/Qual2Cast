[P2.3] RB Global, Inc. keeps the world's assets working. A leading marketplace connecting buyers and sellers of commercial assets, vehicles and real estate, RB Global has a vision to be the world's most trusted asset marketplace. Through its global network of physical locations, digital marketplaces and trusted brands, the company provides transaction solutions, services and insights to customers across construction, commercial transportation, automotive, agriculture, government surplus, lifting and material handling, energy and mining, delivering liquidity, trust and ease at every stage of an asset's life. Its brands include Ritchie Bros., IAA, BigIron, Rouse Services, SmartEquip and VeriTread.
[P2.4] Wherever the equipment sits, the world can bid on it. RB Global runs a network of full-service yards across multiple countries, each staffed with on-site experts who know their regions and their equipment, layered with a digital platform reaching buyers in multiple countries.
[P2.5] RB Global's family of brands spans every asset class and every stage of the lifecycle, from heavy equipment and total-loss vehicles to market data and parts management.
[P2.6] The world's largest marketplace for heavy equipment, trucks, and commercial assets, spanning unreserved auctions, online sales, and negotiated transactions.
[P2.7] Data-driven market intelligence and performance benchmarking for equipment owners, lenders, and fleet managers.
[P2.8] Connecting equipment owners, dealers, and OEMs for smarter parts procurement and service management.
[P2.9] Providing strategic oversight, governance, and long-term vision to ensure sustainable growth and corporate accountability across all global operations.
[P2.10] brothers Dave, Ken, and John Ritchie held an auction to clear surplus furniture from their store in Kelowna, British Columbia, and pay off a bank note. It worked so well that the brothers founded Ritchie Bros. Auction Galleries Ltd., the start of what would grow into the world's largest marketplace for heavy equipment.
[P2.11] Dave ran the company's first equipment auction in Radium Hot Springs. When rain threatened to shut it down, he and his brothers moved the sale under a shop's eaves and drove each piece of equipment past bidders one at a time, an improvised fix that became the "ramp-and-stage" method used at auctions for decades to come. His insistence on unreserved auctions, where the highest bid always wins, and his belief in treating customers like friends shaped the culture that carried Ritchie Bros. from a single yard in Kelowna to a global platform.
[P2.12] That same platform now connects buyers and sellers across the world, offering more ways to sell than Dave could have imagined from that first Kelowna storefront, but still running on the same idea: let the market set the price, every time.
