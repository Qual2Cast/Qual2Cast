You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: HireQuest, Inc. (hirequest.com)
- Core offering: Franchisor of local staffing offices that place temporary and direct-hire blue-collar workers with businesses. Franchisees pay royalties and fees, while the company supplies the brand, systems and training.
- Primary products: none
- Primary services: temporary staffing; direct-hire placement; payroll services for temporary workers
- Customer segment: B2B: businesses needing light industrial, manufacturing, warehouse and hospitality labor
- End markets: light industrial; manufacturing; warehousing and logistics; construction; hospitality
- Revenue model: licensing or royalties: franchise royalties on staffing revenue earned by franchisee offices; other: initial franchise fees and other brand and system fees paid by franchisees
- Value chain position: service provider: places temporary and direct-hire workers with business customers, sold through franchisee offices; other: franchisor collecting royalties from independently owned staffing offices
- Geography served: US national: franchised staffing offices across many US states
- Product specialization: niche specialist: blue-collar and light industrial staffing franchises
- Distribution channels: partners / resellers: independently owned franchisee staffing offices
- Regulatory exposure: medium: employment and wage-and-hour rules from the US Department of Labor and state agencies, and franchise sales rules from the FTC
