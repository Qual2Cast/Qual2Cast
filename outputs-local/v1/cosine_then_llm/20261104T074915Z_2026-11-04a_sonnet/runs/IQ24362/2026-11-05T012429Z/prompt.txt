You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

- Primary products: hospital antibiotics; critical care vasopressor
- Primary services: none
- Customer segment: B2B: hospitals and drug wholesalers buying specialty drugs, pharmaceutical partners paying royalties
- End markets: healthcare; hospitals; respiratory medicine
- Revenue model: licensing or royalties: royalties on respiratory drugs sold by pharmaceutical partners; product sales: antibiotics and critical care drugs sold to hospitals
- Value chain position: brand owner: commercializes specialty hospital drugs, sold to hospitals and wholesalers; other: owns royalty interests in respiratory drugs made and sold by partners
- Geography served: global: US hospitals for product sales, royalties on partner sales in many countries
- Product specialization: related lines: respiratory drug royalty interests, hospital antibiotics, critical care drug
- Distribution channels: distributors / dealers: drug wholesalers supplying hospitals; direct sales: specialty sales force calling on hospitals; other: royalty payments received from pharmaceutical partners
- Regulatory exposure: high: FDA approval needed to sell drugs in the US, royalty revenue depends on partners' approvals
