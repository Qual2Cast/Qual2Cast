You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: Dave Inc. (dave.com)
- Core offering: Mobile banking app and checking account for US consumers living paycheck to paycheck, with small cash advances, budgeting tools and bill alerts. Revenue comes mainly from monthly memberships and fees on instant transfers and cash advances, with banking services provided through a partner bank.
- Primary products: checking account; cash advances; budgeting tools
- Primary services: mobile banking; bill and fee management; financial coaching
- Customer segment: B2C: individual consumers, mainly lower-income workers paid on a regular schedule
- End markets: consumer banking; personal finance; fintech
- Revenue model: subscription: monthly membership fee for app features and banking access; transaction or usage fees: fees for instant transfers and expedited cash advances
- Value chain position: software / platform: mobile app and banking platform, sold to individual consumers; financial intermediary: offers deposit accounts and cash advances through a partner bank to individual consumers
- Geography served: US national: consumers across all 50 US states
- Product specialization: niche specialist: one consumer banking app line covering checking accounts and cash advances
- Distribution channels: online: mobile app and website, downloaded through app stores
- Regulatory exposure: high: consumer banking and cash advances under state money transmitter licenses and CFPB and state consumer finance rules
