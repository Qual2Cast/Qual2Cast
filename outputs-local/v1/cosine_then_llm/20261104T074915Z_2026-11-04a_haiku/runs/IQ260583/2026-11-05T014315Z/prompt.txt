You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: Chemed Corporation (www.chemed.com)
- Core offering: Provides hospice end-of-life care to terminally ill patients and families through its hospice unit, and residential and commercial plumbing, drain cleaning and sewer services through its plumbing unit. Hospice is paid mainly by government programs, while plumbing is sold to homeowners and businesses.
- Primary products: water heaters; plumbing fixtures; drain cleaning equipment
- Primary services: hospice care; drain cleaning; plumbing repair; sewer services; water heater installation
- Customer segment: B2C: hospice patients and their families, homeowners needing plumbing service; B2B: commercial property owners and businesses needing plumbing service; B2G: Medicare and Medicaid programs paying for hospice care
- End markets: healthcare; residential home services; commercial property maintenance
- Revenue model: healthcare reimbursement: Medicare and Medicaid payments for hospice care; service fees: plumbing and drain service calls and repairs; product sales: water heaters, fixtures and parts sold with installations; licensing or royalties: franchise fees and royalties from independent plumbing franchisees
- Value chain position: service provider: takes in patient referrals and service requests, sells hospice care and plumbing services; brand owner: franchises its plumbing brand to independent operators who pay royalties
- Geography served: US national: hospice programs in many states and plumbing operations across the United States
- Product specialization: diversified: hospice care and plumbing and drain services, separate businesses with different customers
- Distribution channels: own locations: company-owned hospice offices and plumbing service branches; partners / resellers: independent plumbing franchisees operating under the company brand; direct sales: physician and hospital referrals for hospice, direct booking by homeowners for plumbing
- Regulatory exposure: high: hospice needs Medicare certification and state licensing, plumbing is subject to local licensing and plumbing codes
