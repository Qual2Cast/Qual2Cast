You help a private-equity analyst choose comparable public companies (comps) for valuing a target company. A buyout investor values a target from the EV/EBITDA multiples of companies in the same business: the same main products or services, sold to the same kind of customers, earning money the same way. Scale matters less than being in the same business.

Below are the target and a list of candidate comps, each with its name, website and a description of its business model. Use the descriptions and what you know about the companies.

For every candidate, decide whether it is a good comp for the target:
- good: in the same business as the target, so its multiple says something about the target's.
- bad: a different business, even if it shares an industry label, customers or a technology.

Rules:
- Answer for every candidate, once, by its label (C1, C2, ...).
- The reason is one short sentence, at most twenty words, naming what matches or differs.
- Judge the business only. Do not use or mention valuations, multiples, share prices or financial figures.

Target: Cirrus Logic, Inc. (www.cirrus.com)
- Core offering: Designs fabless mixed-signal and audio integrated circuits, such as audio converters, amplifiers and haptic drivers, sold to device makers. Customers build them into phones, laptops, earbuds and other consumer and automotive products.
- Primary products: audio codecs; audio amplifiers; haptic drivers; wireless charging chips; sensor interface chips
- Primary services: none
- Customer segment: B2B: electronics device makers, contract manufacturers, automotive electronics suppliers
- End markets: smartphones; laptops and PCs; wearables and earbuds; automotive; consumer electronics
- Revenue model: product sales: chips sold to device makers and through distributors; licensing or royalties: software licenses for audio processing algorithms
- Value chain position: component supplier: designs chips that are built by outside foundries and sold to device makers for integration
- Geography served: global: sales to customers in Asia, Europe and North America, with main design and sales offices worldwide
- Product specialization: related lines: audio, haptics, sensing and power chips sharing mobile and consumer electronics customers
- Distribution channels: direct sales: sales teams selling to major device makers; distributors / dealers: independent electronic component distributors
- Regulatory exposure: medium: export controls on semiconductor technology and environmental rules on chip materials, such as RoHS compliance
