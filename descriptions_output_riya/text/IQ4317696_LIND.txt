The Expeditions That Started It All explorer Lars-Eric Lindblad brought the first tourists to Antarctica on a pioneering journey that laid the groundwork for modern expedition cruising. The following year, he led the first international travelers to the Galápagos Islands—another landmark voyage that helped shape this new era of exploration. Widely regarded as the father of ecotourism, Lars-Eric saw travel not simply as a way to see the world, but as a way to change how people think about it. By opening these remote regions to thoughtful exploration, he demonstrated that travel could be a powerful force for learning and discovery, bringing people closer to wild places and the stories they hold. Lars-Eric’s son, Sven Lindblad, founded [COMPANY] to carry his father’s pioneering vision forward. He built a fleet designed to explore the world’s remote places, expanding the company’s reach to Baja California, the High Arctic and beyond. Under his leadership, [COMPANY] refined the model Lars-Eric first introduced, pairing expeditions guided by experts with itineraries designed to foster a deeper understanding of the planet. In this short film, Sven reflects on his father’s groundbreaking journey and the enduring purpose that continues to inspire new generations of explorers.
