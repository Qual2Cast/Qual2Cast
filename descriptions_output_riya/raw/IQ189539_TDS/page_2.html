1969

LeRoy T. Carlson founds the company with 10 small, rural telephone operations in Wisconsin.

1970

LeRoy Carlson, foreseeing the critical role of data, renames the company Telephone and Data Systems, Inc.

1974

TDS grows to 39 companies in 17 states, and pays its first dividend.

1975

TDS reaches 100,000 subscribers.

1976

TDS acquires Illinois printing company Suttle Press and relocates it to Wisconsin.

1979

TDS begins its second decade with 47 local offices and more than 170,000 telephones in service.

1981

TDS is listed on the American Stock Exchange under the ticker ‘TDS’, selling one million shares.

LeRoy T. Carlson, Jr. becomes president of TDS. LeRoy T. Carlson remains chief executive officer and chairman of the TDS board of directors.

1983

U.S. Cellular is founded as a cellular service subsidiary of TDS.

1986

LeRoy T. Carlson, Jr. becomes president and chief executive officer of TDS.

1988

U.S. Cellular becomes a public company, traded on the American Stock Exchange under the stock ticker ‘USM’.

TDS forms TDS Telecommunications Corporation (TDS Telecom) as its fully owned wireline service provider.

1989

TDS reaches its 20th year with 74 local offices in 27 states serving 264,000 access lines. U.S. Cellular is the nation’s eighth-largest cellular company.

1990

TDS Telecom names James Barr III president and CEO.

1993

TDS has more than one million customers from all of its subsidiaries.

1994

TDS Telecom acquires long-distance telephone company USLink.

1995

U.S. Cellular grows to 500,000 subscribers and 1,000 cell sites.

1996

U.S. Cellular reaches one million subscribers.

Aerial Communications, a TDS subsidiary, goes public in an initial public offering.

 

The Telecommunications Act of 1996 introduces the Competitive Local Exchange Carrier (CLEC) concept, allowing new CLECs to compete against Incumbent Local Exchange Carriers (ILECs), using certain network elements of the local incumbent provider. Rural ILECs such as TDS Telecom are excluded from having to provide CLEC access to their network elements.

1997

TDS Telecom begins offering DISH NetworkTM satellite TV service.

1998

U.S. Cellular reaches two million customers and 2,000 cell sites.

TDS Telecom begins operating a CLEC subsidiary, TDS Metrocom.

2000

TDS Telecom introduces digital subscriber line (DSL) service.

U.S. Cellular names John E. “Jack” Rooney president and CEO.

 

U.S. Cellular reaches three million customers.

Aerial Communications is merged into VoiceStream.

2001

TDS Telecom incorporates Chorus Communications Group.

Suttle Press acquires Straus Printing Company and becomes Suttle-Straus, Inc.

2002

Walter C.D. Carlson is appointed (non-executive) chairman of the board of TDS, and LeRoy T. Carlson becomes chairman emeritus of the TDS board of directors.

TDS Telecom acquires Merrimack County Telephone, Inc. and Telecommunication Systems of New Hampshire, Inc.

 

U.S. Cellular enters the Chicago market after acquiring the Chicago metropolitan trading area (MTA).

U.S. Cellular reaches four million customers and 3,000 cell sites.

2003

TDS celebrates 35 years of providing outstanding communications experiences.

U.S. Cellular acquires naming rights to the Chicago White Sox ball park, which becomes U.S. Cellular Field.

2004

TDS Telecom’s DSL market share in its ILEC markets surpasses that of cable modem after only four years of offering DSL.

2005

U.S. Cellular reaches five million customers.

TDS pays a special common share dividend, doubling the number of shares of TDS outstanding common stock.

2006

TDS Telecom reaches 100,000 DSL customers.

2007

TDS Telecom names David R. Wittwer president and CEO; James Barr III retires.

U.S. Cellular reaches six million customers.

2008

TDS and U.S. Cellular list on the New York Stock Exchange, retaining their ticker symbols.

TDS Telecom introduces managedIP, a hosted Internet Protocol (IP) solution for businesses, and launches TDS TV® in two trial markets in Tennessee.

TDS founder LeRoy T. Carlson becomes director emeritus of TDS, a non-voting position.

2009

U.S. Cellular receives its seventh-consecutive J.D. Power and Associates award for call quality in the North Central Region.

TDS Telecom is approved for $136 million in government stimulus funding to bring broadband access to rural areas.

2010

U.S. Cellular names Mary N. Dillon president and CEO; Jack Rooney retires.

U.S. Cellular offers Android-powered smartphones and tablet, and launches an exclusive package of benefits and services called The Belief ProjectSM.

 

U.S. Cellular receives its tenth-consecutive J.D. Power and Associates awards for call quality in the North Central Region, and wins the PC Magazine Readers’ Choice Award for wireless carriers with a contract option for the third time.

TDS builds its hosted and managed services business with the acquisitions of data center services and managed hosting providers VISI Incorporated of Minnesota and TEAM Technologies, LLC of Iowa and Wisconsin.

 

TDS Telecom improves service reliability and increases data capacity with the first phase of its 10-gigabyte network.

U.S. Cellular and TDS are named among the 200 “Most Trustworthy Companies” in the U.S. by Forbes.

TDS founder LeRoy T. Carlson is inducted into the Wireless History Foundation Hall of Fame at age 94.

2011

U.S. Cellular and TDS Telecom are named J.D. Power and Associates 2011 Customer Service Champions.

TDS acquires Arizona-based OneNeck IT Services Corporation, a provider of hosted application management and managed IT hosting services. OneNeck is managed by TDS Hosted & Managed Services, LLC.

U.S. Cellular wins its second-consecutive PC Magazine Readers’ Choice Award for wireless carriers that offer a contract option. U.S. Cellular has won the award four times in the past five years.

2012

TDS, parent company to TDS Telecom, acquires Vital Support Systems, LLC. This acquisition brings deep IT Solution Provider capabilities to TDS Hosted & Managed Services, LLC.

U.S. Cellular’s outstanding customer service continues to receive third-party recognition: J.D. Power and Associates 2012 Customer Service Champion; Highest Network Quality Performance Among Wireless Cell Phone Users in the North Central Region, J.D. Power and Associates; top smartphone contract carrier in PC Magazine Readers’ Choice Awards.

 

TDS is once again among Forbes’ 200 “Most Trustworthy Companies” in the U.S.

U.S. Cellular begins offering prepaid and postpaid wireless service through Walmart stores nationwide.

 

TDS adds Vital Support Systems, an IT solution provider, to the TDS Hosted & Managed Services business. Later in 2012, Vital launches ReliaCloud, a highly secure enterprise cloud solution housed in Tier III data centers. TEAM Technologies, acquired in 2010, becomes part of Vital Support Systems.

U.S. Cellular agrees to sell its Chicago, St. Louis, central Illinois and three other Midwest markets to Sprint, to increase focus on markets where it has strong positions.

 

TDS TV, TDS Telecom’s proprietary IPTV service, is available in 10 markets by the end of 2012.

U.S. Cellular brings 4G LTE network access to 61 percent of its customers, and introduces iconic new 4G LTE smartphones, tablets and mobile hotspots. U.S. Cellular also receives up to $40.1 million in funds from the FCC’s Sept. 27 Mobility Auction 901 to bring mobile broadband service to rural communities in 10 states.

2013

Phil LaForge is named president of TDS Hosted & Managed Services.

TDS agrees to acquire Baja Broadband, a cable company headquartered in New Mexico, to support TDS Telecom’s broadband and video growth strategy.

 

Cecilia D. Stewart is appointed to the U.S. Cellular Board of Directors.

U.S. Cellular announces that it will begin offering Apple products later in 2013.

 

U.S. Cellular closes the transaction to divest its Chicago, St. Louis, central Illinois and three other markets for $480 million. U.S. Cellular later declares a special cash dividend of $5.75 per Common Share and Series A Common Share, for an aggregate amount of approximately $481 million.

U.S. Cellular names Kenneth R. Meyers president and CEO of U.S. Cellular; Mary Dillon resigns.

 

U.S. Cellular agrees to sell the majority of its Mississippi Valley 10MHz REA AWS spectrum license for $308 million, subject to regulatory approval. The transaction is expected to close in 2013.

TDS Telecom brings its TDS® FibervilleTM marketing campaign to communities in its markets to create awareness of services like 100Mbps Internet, high-definition TDS TV® and digital voice.

 

TDS closes the Baja Broadband acquisition and begins the integration process.

TDS expands the solution provider capabilities of its hosted and managed services business through the acquisition of MSN Communications, Inc., headquartered in Colorado. MSN Communications becomes part of TDS Hosted & Managed Services, operated by TDS Telecom.

2014

U.S. Cellular is named a 2014 J.D. Power Customer Champion, one of only 50 companies nationwide to be recognized for service excellence.

TDS Hosted & Managed Services rebrands as OneNeck® IT Solutions, incorporating all of the existing TDS HMS companies (Vital Support Systems, OneNeck IT Services Corp., VISI, and MSN Communications) under the new brand. Phil LaForge is named president and CEO of OneNeck IT Solutions.

 

TDS acquires BendBroadband, a cable and broadband company headquartered in Bend, Oregon, and its two affiliated businesses, The Vault™ and Zolo Media.

Telephone and Data Systems rebrands as TDS and retires the telephone logo. The new brand provides a strong and open platform for TDS' future growth.

 

Donald C. Nebergall resigns from the TDS Board of Directors and becomes a Director Emeritus of TDS.

David A. Wittwer is appointed to the TDS Board of Directors.

Steven T. Campbell; Peter L. Sereda; Douglas D. Shuma and Kurt B. Thaus are appointed to U.S. Cellular Board of Directors.

2015

U.S. Cellular completes 4G LTE deployment providing customers in more remote rural areas, with all of the benefits of U.S. Cellular’s data services and creating new growth opportunities for the Company.

OneNeck IT Solutions opens data center in Denver, Colorado and expands another in Madison, Wisconsin creating additional capacity and poising the company for growth.

TDS Telecom continues to deploy fiber technology in key markets, reaching 21% of Ilec service addresses.

2016

Founder and longtime chairman of TDS, LeRoy T. Carlson passes away at 100.

TDS is named one of the Best Places to Work in Madison, Wisconsin by Madison Magazine.

 

U.S. Cellular and TDS named to Best Places to Work by Forbes Magazine.

U.S. Cellular awarded “Highest Wireless Network Quality Performance in the North Central Region” by J.D. Power.

2017

Kim Dixon is appointed to the TDS Board of Directors.

For the 19th time since 2006 U.S. Cellular awarded “Highest Wireless Network Quality Performance in the North Central Region” by J.D. Power.

OneNeck IT Solutions names Terry Swanson president and CEO; Phil LaForge resigns.

2018

TDS Telecom names James Butman president and CEO; David Wittwer retires, but remains on the TDS Board of Directors.
