Our commitment to patient privacy & data ethics

At Labcorp, protecting patient privacy is at the core of everything we do. All data powering our real-world evidence and analytics solutions is carefully de-identified in compliance with HIPAA, GDPR and other global privacy standards. We never share personally identifiable information (PII) or protected health information (PHI) without explicit patient consent.

Our data governance framework ensures every dataset meets the highest ethical and regulatory standards through:

Comprehensive de-identification & tokenization preventing re-identification of individuals

Patient consent management aligned with global regulatory and ethical requirements

Independent oversight & compliance reviews applied to every partnership and use case

Transparency & accountability in how data is collected, connected, and applied to research

Frequently asked questions

Real-world data (RWD) refers to health-related information routinely collected during the delivery of healthcare. Common sources include electronic health records (EHRs), insurance claims, laboratory results, registries, and digital health technologies.
RWD reflects how patients are diagnosed, treated, and managed in real-world clinical practice.

Was this Helpful?

Real-world evidence (RWE) is the clinical insight generated through the systematic analysis of RWD.

While RWD represents the raw healthcare data, RWE represents the conclusions and findings derived from rigorous analytical methods applied to that data. These insights can inform regulatory decisions, clinical guidelines, reimbursement strategies, and development planning.

Was this Helpful?

Traditional clinical trial data is generated in controlled research environments with defined protocols and selected patient populations.

In contrast:

  • RWD reflects routine care across diverse patient populations
  • RWE provides insights derived from analyzing that data
  • Clinical trials measure safety and efficacy under controlled conditions
  • RWE helps assess effectiveness and utilization in real-world settings

Both approaches are complementary within evidence generation strategies.

Was this Helpful?

By analyzing real-world data sources such as laboratory results, electronic health records, and claims data, researchers can strengthen patient recruitment strategies by:

  • Identifying patients who meet complex eligibility criteria based on real-world diagnostic and treatment patterns
  • Estimating the size of eligible populations within defined geographic regions
  • Understanding how patients move through diagnostic pathways prior to study enrollment
  • Anticipating potential enrollment barriers based on real-world care patterns

These insights help sponsors align recruitment outreach with routine clinical practice, reducing uncertainty around enrollment timelines and improving recruitment efficiency.

More information on how real-world datasets support feasibility and patient identification is available through Labcorp’s Real-World Data Solutions platform.

Real-world evidence allows researchers to analyze how therapies are prescribed, adjusted, and monitored across large patient populations in routine clinical care.

By studying longitudinal healthcare data, researchers can evaluate:

  • Treatment initiation patterns
  • Therapy switching or discontinuation trends
  • Adherence and persistence over time
  • Real-world clinical outcomes across patient subgroups

These insights help healthcare stakeholders better understand how diseases are managed in practice and identify potential gaps in care.

For biopharma researchers, treatment pattern analysis can inform clinical development strategy, identify unmet needs, and support post-market research.

Large datasets combining laboratory diagnostics, claims information, and clinical records can provide a more comprehensive view of patient journeys and disease progression.

Real-world evidence provides insights into how therapies perform across broader patient populations and clinical environments. These insights can help clinicians, researchers, payers, and policymakers better understand treatment effectiveness outside controlled clinical trial settings.
Real-world evidence can support:

  • Evaluation of long-term treatment outcomes
  • Identification of patient subgroups that respond differently to therapies
  • Assessment of safety signals and adverse events in larger populations
  • Evidence generation for clinical guidelines and reimbursement decisions

By analyzing data from routine clinical care, healthcare stakeholders can make more informed decisions about treatment strategies and care pathways.

In certain disease areas, including neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease, real-world datasets can also provide insights into diagnostic patterns and disease progression. Was this Helpful?

Real-world data can provide valuable insights into disease prevalence, diagnostic timelines, and treatment pathways before a clinical trial begins.

These insights can help researchers refine clinical trial protocols by:

  • Evaluating the feasibility of inclusion and exclusion criteria
  • Identifying appropriate endpoints based on real-world clinical practice
  • Estimating patient availability across geographic regions
  • Understanding diagnostic and treatment pathways prior to enrollment

By grounding trial design decisions in real-world patient journeys, sponsors may reduce protocol amendments and improve operational efficiency.

Real-world datasets that include diagnostic laboratory data can be particularly valuable in understanding how diseases are identified and managed across healthcare systems.

Real-world data (RWD) comes from a wide range of sources that capture how healthcare is delivered and experienced outside of controlled clinical trials. The most commonly used types of RWD include claims data, electronic health record (EHR) data and laboratory data, which together provide important insights into patient care, treatment patterns and outcomes. However, RWD can also originate from other sources such as disease registries, social determinants of health (SDOH) data, clinical notes, patient-reported outcomes, and other healthcare datasets. Each data source offers a different lens into the patient journey, and when combined, they can provide a more comprehensive view of real-world healthcare.

Claims Data

Claims datasets capture billing and insurance transactions associated with healthcare services.

They typically include:

  • Diagnoses and procedure codes
  • Medication prescriptions and pharmacy claims
  • Healthcare utilization information

Claims data is often used to study healthcare utilization patterns and cost outcomes.

Electronic Health Record (EHR) Data

EHR data contains clinical information recorded during patient encounters.
Examples include:

  • Physician notes
  • Diagnoses and clinical observations
  • Vital signs and medications

EHR datasets provide clinical context but may vary in completeness and standardization.

Laboratory Real-World Data

Laboratory datasets capture diagnostic testing results and biomarker information.

These datasets can provide insights into:

  • Diagnostic pathways
  • Disease biomarkers
  • Disease progression markers

Because diagnostic testing often occurs early in the patient journey, laboratory real-world data can provide unique insights into disease identification and stratification.

Integrated datasets combining claims, EHR, and laboratory information can provide a more comprehensive view of patient journeys across the healthcare system.

Was this Helpful?

Real-world data can support multiple stages of the drug development lifecycle, from early research through post-market evaluation.

Common applications include:

Early Research

  • Understanding disease prevalence and patient populations
  • Identifying potential therapeutic targets

Clinical Development

  • Trial feasibility and site selection
  • Patient identification and recruitment planning

Regulatory and Market Access

  • Generating real-world evidence to support regulatory submissions
  • Supporting health economics and outcomes research (HEOR)

Post-Market Research

  • Monitoring long-term safety and effectiveness
  • Evaluating real-world treatment outcomes

As the healthcare ecosystem generates increasing volumes of clinical data, real-world evidence continues to play an expanding role in informing drug development and healthcare decision-making.

Was this Helpful?

Diagnostic patterns observed in real-world clinical data can help researchers identify distinct patient subgroups within broader disease populations.

By analyzing diagnostic testing data and longitudinal clinical records, researchers may identify:

  • Biomarker-defined patient subsets
  • Disease progression patterns
  • Diagnostic triggers and timelines
  • Differences in treatment response across patient groups

These insights can help support patient stratification strategies, which are increasingly important for precision medicine and targeted therapies.

For example, in neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease, real-world datasets can provide insights into biomarker testing patterns, disease staging, and patient identification strategies. More information is available through Labcorp’s Alzheimer’s real-world data cohort.
